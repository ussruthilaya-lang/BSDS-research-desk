# 10. Team Responsibilities & Conflict Resolution

**10. Responsibilities and conflict resolution.** The work is divided into three lanes, and the handoff files in the repository list every task with its target date.

| Member | Responsibilities | Leads |
|---|---|---|
| Sruthilaya | Job model, subagent runner, cache and merge, the lead's plan and gather | E2 and E3 |
| Satyaa | Provider simulator, gateway, admission control, task leases and crash recovery, the MCP server and the demonstration | E4 |
| Muskan | Job API, workers, Docker Compose, replay harness, observability, live control room, AWS deployment | E1 and the simulator-versus-deployed check |

Each member writes the report sections for their own lane and experiment. Satyaa assembles the HW9 proposal and leads the final presentation, and Muskan lays out the poster. A change to the shared contract needs all three members to agree.

If a conflict arises, the team answers the three questions this way:

- **How are teamwork issues addressed?** Whoever notices the issue raises it in the group chat within one day, describing the task and its effect and not the person. If the chat does not settle it, the team meets in person or on a video call within two days, decides, and writes the decision in the project-management log.
- **What happens if a member contributes too little?** First, a teammate asks privately what is blocking them and offers help. If a target date passes with no update, the task moves to that lane's backup and the team meets to rebalance the work. Pull requests and the log show who has delivered what, so the discussion rests on the record.
- **When is the instructor involved?** The team writes to the instructor together if the issue is still unresolved 48 hours after a team meeting, or if it puts a graded deadline at risk that the other two members cannot cover. The log serves as the record.
